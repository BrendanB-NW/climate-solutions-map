## The graveyard — what failure teaches about white space

| Company | Sector | What they attempted | What happened (year) | Signal for the field |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Northvolt | Batteries | Europe's homegrown gigafactory champion to rival CATL/BYD, vertically integrated from cathode to recycling | Filed for bankruptcy in Sweden (Mar 2025) with ~$5.8B debt after raising ~$12B; had lost a €2B BMW order over delivery delays [1][2] | Scale-up execution, not cell chemistry, is the kill zone; parallel megaprojects on venture/debt capital outran operating capability |
| Moxion Power | Batteries | Mobile battery units to replace diesel generators on film sets, festivals, construction | Abrupt shutdown (Jul 2024) after raising $110M+; scaled manufacturing before product validation [3] | Hardware that skips iteration to chase manufacturing scale dies of its own capex; "demand anticipated, not validated" |
| Ambri | Batteries | Liquid-metal long-duration storage (MIT spinout, Gates-backed) | Chapter 11 (May 2024); assets sold to a lender consortium incl. Gates Frontier and Paulson (Jul 2024) [4] | Novel chemistries can't outrun lithium-ion's cost curve; survival came only via creditor takeover, not markets |
| Natron Energy | Batteries | Sodium-ion cells for data centers and industrial power | Ceased operations (Sep 2025), closing its $40M Michigan plant and shelving gigafactory plans [5] | Alt-chemistry needs committed anchor offtake before factory buildout; policy/tariff whiplash removed the bridge |
| Powin | Grid storage | Third-largest US battery energy storage (BESS) integrator | Chapter 11 (Jun 2025), eight months after a $200M KKR credit facility [6] | Integrators are margin-squeezed between Chinese cell makers and EPCs; tariff uncertainty broke the model |
| Lordstown Motors | EVs | Electric pickup (Endurance) built in an ex-GM plant | Chapter 11 (Jun 2023) after Hindenburg exposed faked reservations and the Foxconn deal collapsed [7] | SPAC-era "reservations" were demand fiction; shell survived only as a litigation vehicle |
| Proterra | EVs | Electric transit buses plus battery systems and charging | Chapter 11 (Aug 2023) after aggressive three-business expansion drained capital [7] | Running multiple capital-hungry lines on municipal sales cycles is incompatible with venture timelines |
| Volta Trucks | EVs | Electric 16-tonne urban delivery trucks (Sweden/UK) | Bankruptcy (Oct 2023) triggered by battery supplier Proterra's collapse; relaunched, then failed a second time (2025) [8][9] | Failure cascades through immature supply chains — one node's bankruptcy kills its customers |
| Arrival | EVs | Electric vans built in low-capex "microfactories" | Insolvency (2024) without ever making a commercial sale; assets sold to Canoo [7] | Novel product plus novel manufacturing process compounds risk instead of hedging it |
| Fisker | EVs | Asset-light EV brand (Ocean SUV contract-built by Magna) | Chapter 11 (Jun 2024) after demand, software and service failures; no automaker rescue came [7] | Outsourcing the factory doesn't outsource the hard parts: software, quality, distribution, demand |
| Canoo | EVs | Modular electric vans (NASA, USPS, military pilots) | Chapter 7 liquidation (Jan 2025) with ~$700K in the bank [10] | Pilot deals with marquee logos are not a business; straight-to-liquidation signals no salvageable core |
| Tritium | EV charging | DC fast-charger manufacturing (~13,000 chargers, 47 countries) | Went bust (Apr 2024); bought by India's Exicom [11] | Even category leaders in charging hardware couldn't reach margin; consolidation into strategics, not IPOs |
| Lilium | Electric aviation | eVTOL "electric jet" for regional air mobility | Insolvency (late 2024) after ~€1.5B spent; €200M rescue collapsed, second filing ended it (Feb 2025) [12] | Certification-grade aerospace exceeds what venture + SPAC capital can carry; no revenue before ~$2B spend |
| Nikola | Hydrogen | Fuel-cell semi trucks plus a hydrogen fueling network | Chapter 11 (Feb 2025) with >$1B liabilities after founder's fraud conviction; workforce fell to one [13][14] | Hype-first capital formation, plus chicken-and-egg fueling: ~6 heavy-duty H2 stations existed in the US |
| Hyzon Motors | Hydrogen | Fuel-cell heavy trucks (refuse trucks as beachhead) | Shareholders voted to dissolve; wound down (early 2025) [14] | Without fuel infrastructure, FCEV trucks had no TCO case against diesel or battery-electric |
| Quantron / HyVia | Hydrogen | German FCEV trucks; Renault–Plug Power JV for H2 vans | Quantron insolvent (2024); HyVia wound down (2025) [14] | Even OEM-backed hydrogen mobility folded — the H2 transport niche collapsed across an entire cohort |
| Universal Hydrogen | Hydrogen | Modular hydrogen capsules and conversion kits for regional aircraft | Shut down (mid-2024) after burning ~$100M; flew a testbed but couldn't raise more [11][15] | "Picks and shovels" still dies if the ecosystem never arrives; flight demos don't bridge to certification money |
| First Mode | Hydrogen | Hydrogen-hybrid retrofits for ultra-class mining haul trucks | Chapter 11 (Dec 2024) with ~$98M debt after patron Anglo American pulled funding; Cummins bought the assets (2025) [16][17] | Single-customer/patron dependence; incumbents wait and buy the tech in distress rather than fund the startup |
| Running Tide | Carbon removal | Ocean CDR — sinking limestone-coated biomass and growing kelp | Shut down (Jun 2024) despite Microsoft, Shopify and Stripe deals and $50M+ raised; MRV credibility was contested [18][19] | Voluntary carbon demand is too thin and quality-sensitive to finance first-of-a-kind ocean science |
| Nori | Carbon markets / software | Marketplace for farmer-generated soil carbon credits ($17M raised) | Shut down (2024), citing a stagnant voluntary carbon market [20] | Marketplace software can't fix supply-side credibility or conjure demand in a trust-broken VCM |
| Aspiration | Climate fintech | "Tree-planting" green neobank turned carbon-credit broker to Meta and Microsoft | Bankruptcy (Mar 2025); co-founder pleaded guilty to $248M investor fraud [21][22] | The climate halo can mask fraud longer; counterparty diligence in carbon markets was dangerously shallow |
| Swell Energy | Climate software | Virtual power plant aggregator running utility programs (incl. Hawaiian Electric) | Shut down and liquidated via ABC (late 2024); utility VPP programs paused in the fallout [23][24] | VPP/DER orchestration revenue depends on slow utility programs; grid software gross margins came too late |
| SCiFi Foods | Alt-protein | Cultivated-beef/plant blended burger (a16z-backed, $40M) | Wound down via assignment for benefit of creditors (Jun 2024) [25] | Cultivated meat's cost curve and regulatory pace are incompatible with venture runway lengths |
| Motif FoodWorks | Alt-protein | Food-tech ingredients to make plant-based meat taste better (Ginkgo spinout, ~$345M) | Wound down; assets auctioned (2024) [26] | Upstream ingredient innovation can't save a shrinking end category — plant-based demand collapse moved up the stack |
| Meati Foods | Alt-protein | Mycelium whole-cut "meat" at retail scale (~$450M raised) | Lender swept most of its cash on a technical default; sold for ~$4M via ABC (2025) [26][27] | Venture-backed foodtech with debt is fragile: a covenant breach, not the product, was the proximate killer |
| Bowery Farming | Food & land use | Vertical farming at industrial scale ($700M+ raised, $2.3B valuation) | Ceased all operations (Nov 2024) amid weak premium demand and crop disease [28][29] | Energy and capex per kilogram of greens never beat field agriculture; "software-defined farming" was still a farm |
| Plenty | Food & land use | Vertical farms backed by SoftBank, Walmart and Bezos (~$940M raised) | Chapter 11 (Mar 2025); emerged shrunken, focused on a single strawberry operation [28] | Survival required collapsing to the one high-margin SKU — a verdict on the whole-category thesis |
| SunPower | Solar | Iconic US residential solar installer and financier | Chapter 11 (Aug 2024) despite record US solar installations; key units sold for $45M; delisted [30][11] | Deployment booms don't protect downstream players; rate hikes and net-metering policy shifts crush consumer solar finance |
| Ubiquitous Energy | Solar | Transparent photovoltaic window coatings ($70M+ raised) | Ceased operations (Apr 2024) [11] | Deep-tech materials without a manufacturing partner die in the lab-to-fab scale-up gap |
| Meyer Burger | Solar | Swiss/German cell-and-module manufacturing relocated to the US to chase IRA incentives | German units insolvent (May 2025); US units Chapter 11 (Jun 2025); 900+ layoffs; equipment sold to India's Waaree for $18.5M [31][32] | Western solar manufacturing can't survive Chinese oversupply on incentives alone; subsidy strategy without trade certainty is a trap |

**Failure patterns:**

- **Capital intensity vs. venture timelines.** The biggest corpses — Northvolt (~$12B), Lilium (€1.5B), Plenty ($940M), Bowery ($700M+) — all hit the same wall: first-of-a-kind factories financed on venture equity rather than project finance, with no bridge instrument when milestones slipped. Equity investors fund optionality, not decade-long industrial ramps; the "valley of death" between pilot and bankable plant remains the field's largest unsolved financing gap, and white space exists for whoever structures FOAK debt, insurance, and offtake-backed instruments.
- **Hype cycles and the SPAC-era demand fiction.** The 2020–21 cohort (Nikola, Lordstown, Arrival, Canoo, Fisker, Hyzon, Lilium) raised on reservations, renders and TAM slides; when real orders and gross margins arrived, public markets shut. At the extreme the halo enabled outright fraud — Nikola's Trevor Milton and Aspiration's $248M scheme — suggesting climate branding systematically delayed scrutiny that would have hit a conventional startup sooner [7][13][21].
- **Policy dependence cuts both ways.** SunPower collapsed amid net-metering and rate shifts even as US installations set records; Meyer Burger, Natron and Powin were broken by tariff and IRA-rollback whiplash, part of $24B+ in US clean-energy projects cancelled in 2025 alone [33][34]. Businesses whose unit economics are an incentive arbitrage have no survival margin when rules change mid-build; durable plays must clear market economics with policy as upside, not foundation.
- **Ecosystem chicken-and-egg, supply-chain cascades, and single-patron risk.** Hydrogen trucking died waiting for fueling stations that never came; Running Tide and Nori died waiting for voluntary carbon demand that never matured; Volta Trucks died when its battery supplier Proterra died; First Mode died when one patron (Anglo American) pulled out. Startups whose success requires a second, unbuilt system — or one counterparty — carry compounded risk that venture pricing rarely reflected.
- **Incumbent response: wait, then scavenge.** Asian manufacturers out-scaled Western challengers on cost (Northvolt vs. CATL/BYD, Meyer Burger vs. Chinese modules), and strategics then bought distressed assets for cents on the dollar — Cummins took First Mode, Exicom took Tritium, Waaree took Meyer Burger's equipment, Viridi took Moxion's factory. The lesson for white space: positions that incumbents can simply outwait are not defensible; durable entries pair technology with locked-in demand (offtake, regulated procurement, real software revenue) that an incumbent cannot acquire at the bankruptcy auction.
