## White-space index

Every variation the census flagged as having few credible players, with the researcher's note on why — physics, economics, or incumbency. Raw founder/investor prospecting data; judge each entry critically.

| Sector | Solution area | Variation | Why it's open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buildings | Air-Source Heat Pumps & Electrified HVAC | Air-to-water hydronic heat pumps | Air-to-water is mature in Europe but nearly absent from US distribution despite millions of US hydronic and steam-heated homes — an opening for UL-listed high-temp monoblocs and storage-integrated hydronic packages. |
| Buildings | Air-Source Heat Pumps & Electrified HVAC | Window, room & portable packaged heat pumps (120V saddle units) | Only one venture pure-play (Gradient) exists and its flagship NYCHA rollout has already slowed while Midea's manufacturing scale sets a ~$3k price floor; the opening is a sub-$2,000 saddle unit, tariff-proof domestic assembly, and a landlord/MDU sales channel — the prize is millions of US steam-heated apartments. |
| Buildings | Air-Source Heat Pumps & Electrified HVAC | Commercial rooftop unit (RTU) heat pumps & VRF/VRV systems | Essentially zero startups: AHRI certification, dealer networks, and rooftop curb standards are incumbent moats — the opening is drop-in, curb-compatible cold-climate heat pump RTUs and gas-RTU retrofit kits, which is exactly what DOE's accelerator is trying to pull from incumbents by 2027. |
| Buildings | Air-Source Heat Pumps & Electrified HVAC | High-temperature heat pumps for boiler & steam-system replacement | US steam and pre-war hydronic multifamily has almost no UL-listed high-temperature ASHP options — Europe's 70-75°C R290 monoblocs haven't crossed the Atlantic — leaving Kelvin nearly alone against tens of thousands of NYC-style steam buildings facing Local Law 97-type emissions fines. |
| Buildings | Air-Source Heat Pumps & Electrified HVAC | Natural & low-GWP refrigerant heat pumps (R290, CO2, R32/R454B) | US codes and UL listings still largely block A3 (flammable) refrigerants indoors, so R290 — Europe's winning answer — has no US residential foothold; first movers on US-listed R290 packaged/monobloc designs (charge kept outdoors or hermetically small, e.g. window units) get a multi-year head start when rules loosen. |
| Buildings | Air-Source Heat Pumps & Electrified HVAC | Thermally driven & gas-absorption heat pumps | Sparse by design: at COP ~1.4 on fossil fuel versus 3-4 electric, the niche shrinks as grids decarbonize — it remains credible only where gas/propane infrastructure is sunk and electric capacity is scarce (rural propane markets, demand-constrained urban feeders), so few entrants is rational rather than an opportunity gap. |
| Buildings | Air-Source Heat Pumps & Electrified HVAC | Solid-state & caloric heating/cooling (thermoelectric, elastocaloric, magnetocaloric, barocaloric, thermoacoustic) | Crowded with startups but empty of shipped space-conditioning products — specific cooling power, cyclic fatigue, and cost per kW all still lose to a $300 rotary compressor; the realistic wedge is F-gas phase-down niches (refrigeration, dehumidification) before whole-building HVAC. |
| Buildings | Air-Source Heat Pumps & Electrified HVAC | Ultra-efficient AC & dehumidification (desiccant/evaporative hybrids) + high-efficiency HVAC components | A prior liquid-desiccant wave already died on cost and maintenance (Advantix bankrupt mid-2010s; 7AC's tech absorbed by Emerson in 2019), and Turntide's retreat shows components need OEM channels — the open ground is humid-climate DOAS, grid-interactive cooling with built-in storage, and proving desiccant O&M economics over multi-year fleets. |
| Buildings | Building Envelope: Insulation, Air Sealing & Roofs | Wood-fiber insulation | North America has exactly one producer (TimberHP), and its Chapter 11 shows the capital intensity of mill conversions; as codes start counting embodied carbon, a second NA plant or low-cost import channel is an obvious opening. |
| Buildings | Building Envelope: Insulation, Air Sealing & Roofs | Hemp, straw, sheep's wool, mycelium and other bio-based insulation | Bio-based materials are commercial but a rounding error of the insulation market — fragmented feedstock supply, code approvals and fire/moisture certification keep installed costs high, and Biohm's administration shows the cash-burn risk in mycelium. The opening is code-listed, price-competitive bio-based product in mainstream channels; Hempitecture's DOE-backed Tennessee plant and Havelock's Home Depot listing are the first moves. |
| Buildings | Building Envelope: Insulation, Air Sealing & Roofs | Aerogel and vacuum-insulated panels for space-constrained retrofits | Cost-per-R remains 5-10x bulk insulation, confining aerogel and VIPs to niches; cracking low-cost ambient-pressure aerogel drying (KrossLinker's bet) or VIP edge-loss and puncture durability would open thin-wall urban retrofit at scale. |
| Buildings | Building Envelope: Insulation, Air Sealing & Roofs | Air-sealing technology: aerosolized envelope/duct sealing and membrane/tape systems | Aerosolized interior sealing is effectively a one-company category — Aeroseal holds the LBNL-lineage technology with no scaled competitor; automated or robot-applied sealing for the labor-starved retrofit market is open ground. |
| Buildings | Building Envelope: Insulation, Air Sealing & Roofs | Weatherization diagnostics, tooling and retrofit robotics | Test hardware is a 40-year-old duopoly (Retrotec/TEC); the open ground is the software layer — cheap, automated measured-performance verification (drone thermography, smart-meter-derived heat-loss coefficients) that lets lenders and utilities underwrite retrofit outcomes at portfolio scale. |
| Buildings | Building Envelope: Insulation, Air Sealing & Roofs | Prefabricated panelized deep-retrofit envelopes (Energiesprong-style) | Per-dwelling costs (~€60-100k) still depend on KfW-class subsidies and standardized social-housing stock, so players cluster in NL/DE; no one has industrialized this in the US despite DOE's Advanced Building Construction push — a US integrator pairing prefab overclads with IRA/state retrofit money is wide open. |
| Buildings | Building Envelope: Insulation, Air Sealing & Roofs | Passive radiative-cooling coatings and films | Physics is proven but durability, dirt pickup and $/m² are not solved at commodity scale; the field is seed-stage startups while coatings majors move slowly — Purdue's record 98.1%-reflective barium-sulfate paint is still in tech transfer, so a mass-market cooling paint pushed through Sherwin-Williams/PPG-class distribution is the opening. |
| Buildings | Geothermal, Thermal Networks & District Energy | Residential geothermal developers & drilling cost reduction | Retrofit drilling economics remain brutal — rig mobilization and per-foot costs dwarf the heat pump itself, which pushed even category leader Dandelion from one-off retrofits to new-build subdivisions where rigs amortize across adjacent lots. Openings: compact low-cost residential rigs and one-day installs, and replicating the Lennar playbook with other production builders in states with stacked geothermal incentives (CO, NY, MA) — almost no other venture-backed residential geothermal developer exists in the US. |
| Buildings | Geothermal, Thermal Networks & District Energy | Networked geothermal & utility thermal energy networks (TENs) | Utility pilots depend on rate-based cost recovery and enabling legislation, and almost no independent thermal-energy-network developer-operators exist in the US yet — an opening for ESCO-style companies that design, build and operate networks for utilities, campuses and municipalities as the multi-state legislative wave (MA, NY, CO, WA, MN and others) creates structural demand. |
| Buildings | Geothermal, Thermal Networks & District Energy | Wastewater & sewage heat recovery | Cheap, proven physics but fragmented adoption: every project needs sewer-utility access agreements and custom engineering, and category leader SHARC's ~$3M revenue after a decade shows how slow sales cycles are. The opening is standardized, code-driven products sold through heat-network developers and mandates (Vancouver-style low-carbon energy systems, Scandinavian and German waste-heat rules). |
| Buildings | Geothermal, Thermal Networks & District Energy | Solar thermal district heating | The Danish home market stalled once utilities pivoted to large heat pumps and pit storage charged with cheap wind power — killing market leader Arcon-Sunmark and forcing Savosolar's restructuring. The remaining opening is hybrid plants (collectors + PTES + heat pumps) in Germany and Eastern Europe, where gas displacement economics and EU funds still favor solar heat. |
| Buildings | Low-Carbon Structural Materials & Industrialized Construction | Engineered bamboo and fast-growing-fiber structural products | Almost no code-approved structural bamboo exists in Western markets: the fiber supply sits in the tropics, ICC/Eurocode certification is slow and expensive, and lamination plants are capital-intensive. The opening is code-certified bamboo studs/panels produced near equatorial fiber with US production-builder distribution, sold on hurricane and fire performance plus carbon — the path BamCore's builder partnerships and Rizome's $100M plant are testing. |
| Buildings | Low-Carbon Structural Materials & Industrialized Construction | Carbon-storing and bio-based boards, blocks and finishes | Distribution is the moat, not chemistry: gypsum and OSB incumbents (USG, Knauf, Saint-Gobain, LP) own contractor channels and price points, so bio-boards historically die in the 'green premium' boutique niche. Plantd's national-builder offtake model (D.R. Horton) is the template — winning one production homebuilder beats a thousand one-off architect specs. |
| Buildings | Low-Carbon Structural Materials & Industrialized Construction | Bio-based and carbon-storing insulation | North American wood-fiber capacity is effectively a one-plant monopoly (TimberHP) versus a dozen mature European mills, and TimberHP's Chapter 11 reflected first-of-a-kind mill-retrofit cost risk rather than demand risk — products kept selling through bankruptcy. The opening: second and third NA plants sited on existing sawmill residue streams, financed with project debt rather than venture equity, plus straw/hemp lines feeding code-friendly batt formats. |
| Buildings | Low-Carbon Structural Materials & Industrialized Construction | Prefab/modular low-energy construction and SIP systems | Vertically integrated factory-builders keep dying in housing downturns (Katerra 2021, Veev 2023, Diamond Age 2024): high fixed factory costs collide with cyclical demand and thin construction margins. Surviving models are capital-light — factory-as-product (Cuby), components sold into existing builder channels (Vantem panels), or incumbent-owned offsite capacity (Lennar absorbing Veev) — leaving an opening for component plays that never carry real-estate risk. |
| Buildings | Low-Carbon Structural Materials & Industrialized Construction | 3D-printed and robotic construction | Single-family economics remain unproven — printing replaces one trade among thirty, and two capex-heavy players failed in 18 months (Diamond Age, Mighty Buildings). The defensible openings are commercial/retail printing where schedule is the value (Alquist-Walmart), printer-as-product OEM models (COBOD), and genuinely low-cement printable mixes, since the material — not the robot — is the carbon story. |
| Buildings | Low-Carbon Structural Materials & Industrialized Construction | Deconstruction, material reuse and circular-construction marketplaces | Economics, not technology, keeps this thin: salvage labor costs more than cheap virgin material in most markets, and reclaimed structural members lack easy re-certification, so the sector leans on mandates (EU taxonomy, Portland-style deconstruction ordinances). Openings: robotic processing that collapses labor cost (Urban Machine's wedge), salvage-to-engineered-product upgrading (Cambium's salvaged-wood CLT), and material passports positioned ahead of EU enforcement deadlines. |
| Buildings | Refrigerants & Commercial Refrigeration | Low-GWP refrigerant chemistry and production (HFOs, low-GWP blends) | No startup layer exists and likely cannot: fluorochemical plants cost hundreds of millions, HFO process IP is locked up by the Chemours-Honeywell patent estate, and growing PFAS/TFA scrutiny of HFOs in Europe makes new fluorinated molecules a liability; the real opening is on the natural-refrigerant side (equipment, not chemistry) and in TFA-risk analytics. |
| Buildings | Refrigerants & Commercial Refrigeration | Refrigerant recovery, reclamation and destruction (incl. carbon-credit-financed models) | The US reclaim market is consolidating around a few incumbents, but recovery rates remain ~5-10% of theoretical and the Global South has almost no collection infrastructure — Recoolit's Google deal shows corporate buyers will fund technician-network models in Article 5 countries, and EPA's 2026-2029 reclaim mandates plus state rules (CARB) create room for tech-enabled aggregation of the fragmented contractor recovery chain. |
| Buildings | Refrigerants & Commercial Refrigeration | Refrigerant leak detection and monitoring hardware | Hardware is incumbent-dominated with essentially no venture-backed entrants — sensing physics (infrared/semiconductor cells) is commoditized and channels run through HVAC distribution — but the EPA's automatic-leak-detection mandate (effective 2026-2027) plus natural-refrigerant safety sensing (CO2/R290 in occupied spaces) creates an opening for cheap networked per-case sensors fused with software analytics, which is currently being attacked from the software side (e.g., Axiom Cloud's sensorless leak detection) rather than by new hardware players. |
| Buildings | Refrigerants & Commercial Refrigeration | Refrigerant compliance and lifecycle management software | The category is fragmented between legacy enterprise tools and tiny contractor apps with no clear venture-scale winner; the Jan 2026 ER&R reporting wave, 2027 ALD mandates and EU F-gas 2024 recast force every operator of >15-lb systems into auditable digital records, an opening for a modern system-of-record that fuses sensor data, work orders and Scope 1 emissions reporting — currently being approached sideways by CMMS (Fexa, Facilio) and monitoring (Axiom) players rather than head-on. |
| Buildings | Refrigerants & Commercial Refrigeration | Solid-state and novel-cycle refrigeration (thermoelectric, magnetocaloric, elastocaloric) | Very few credible players because physics is unforgiving: thermoelectric COP collapses at large temperature lifts, and caloric approaches face expensive materials (rare-earth magnets, NiTi fatigue life) and complex mechanics — Phononic licensing its cold-chain business away after ~$200M invested is strong evidence merchandiser economics remain hard; the opening is premium niches where silence, precision or zero-refrigerant compliance justify a price premium (lab/medical, urban micro-stores, EU F-gas-constrained formats) while materials costs ride down. |
| Buildings | Retrofit Delivery, Electrification Platforms & Financing | Panelized / prefabricated deep energy retrofit (Energiesprong model) | Outside Dutch/German social housing the field is nearly empty — the US has no at-scale panelized-retrofit vendor (NYSERDA's RetrofitNY struggled to get bids that pencil) because fragmented ownership, custom facades and cheap gas stretch paybacks; building-performance-standard fines (LL97/BERDO) plus scan-to-fab mass customization create the opening for a US serial-retrofit integrator. |
| Buildings | Retrofit Delivery, Electrification Platforms & Financing | HVAC contractor software: load calculation, design & sales enablement | The category leader was absorbed by ServiceTitan within two years of seed, signaling consolidation into field-service suites; a standalone wedge remains in automated heat-pump design-to-permit (room-by-room sizing, duct mods, electrical load calc, and rebate paperwork in one pass), which furnace-swap-oriented incumbents still don't serve. |
| Buildings | Retrofit Delivery, Electrification Platforms & Financing | Heat-as-a-service & subscription comfort models | True 'heat by the month' barely exists outside boiler-rental cultures (Germany/UK): homeowner credit risk, weather-dependent savings and long service tails killed US pioneer Sealed's pay-for-savings model, and US TPO leases are only now reaching HVAC via Palmetto — an opening for utility tariffed-on-bill heat or insurer-grade comfort subscriptions in North America. |
| Buildings | Retrofit Delivery, Electrification Platforms & Financing | Electrification workforce training & contractor staffing tech | Training content is abundant but throughput isn't: the real bottleneck is paid on-the-job hours and employer sponsorship, which software alone doesn't fund — an opening for earn-while-you-learn staffing models (OEM- or utility-subsidized apprenticeships) tied to verified install-quality data, especially as Europe's installer academies (Octopus, Aira, Heat Geek) have no US equivalent at scale. |
| Buildings | Smart Controls, Demand Flexibility & Thermal Storage | Meter collars and panel-upgrade-avoidance hardware | Sparse by design: meter-socket devices need utility-by-utility approval and UL certification, a multi-year moat that has left ConnectDER nearly alone in the collar form factor. With ~50M US homes on 100A service and panel upgrades costing $2,500-$10,000, low-cost meter-level interconnection and load management is a large, underserved opening as heat pump/EV adoption collides with service limits. |
| Buildings | Smart Controls, Demand Flexibility & Thermal Storage | Fault detection & diagnostics, submetering and energy analytics | Standalone monitoring keeps getting commoditized: consumer disaggregation is being absorbed into smart meters (Sense's pivot) and CRE analytics into BMS/FDD suites (Carbon Lighthouse's contraction). The open ground is analytics that closes the loop — automatically actuating controls or monetizing flexibility — rather than producing reports. |
| Buildings | Smart Controls, Demand Flexibility & Thermal Storage | Behind-the-meter thermal energy storage (ice and phase-change) | Thermal storage is cheap physics with hard economics: Ice Energy went Chapter 7 even holding utility contracts because project sales were slow and margins thin, and incumbents treat ice as a niche chiller accessory. The opening is financeable, VPP-grade TES with stacked revenues — exactly what Nostromo's DOE-backed, 193-building aggregation model and Axiom's software-only approach are testing — plus heat batteries riding heat-pump electrification in space-constrained homes. |
| Buildings | Smart Controls, Demand Flexibility & Thermal Storage | Grid-interactive water heating (CTA-2045/EcoPort thermal batteries) | Despite being the cheapest flexible storage on the grid, this space has almost no venture activity: water heaters are sold through plumbers on burnout replacement, and the flexibility value accrues to utilities rather than the homeowner (split incentives). State CTA-2045 mandates (WA, OR) and 15-minute wholesale settlement create an opening for fleet aggregators and OEM-embedded software, as consumer retrofit plays (e.g., Aquanta) never reached scale. |
| Buildings | Smart Controls, Demand Flexibility & Thermal Storage | Smart ventilation, HRV/ERV and IAQ-driven HVAC load reduction | Ventilation energy is governed by code minimums rather than economics, so few startups can monetize savings — enVerid is nearly alone in load-reduction hardware and most innovation sits inside European incumbents. Post-COVID IAQ expectations plus electrification (ventilation loads on heat pumps) create an opening for controls that co-optimize air quality and energy, and for US adoption of EU-style demand-controlled ventilation. |
| Buildings | Smart Controls, Demand Flexibility & Thermal Storage | Lighting and plug-load controls (networked LED, occupancy-driven) | Post-LED, lighting-control value consolidated into incumbent ecosystems (Enlighted into Siemens, Digital Lumens into OSRAM), leaving little room for fixture-side startups. The genuine gap is plug loads — now approaching a third of commercial building electricity and largely uncontrolled — where Sapient is among the only venture-backed players. |
| Buildings | Water Heating & Appliance Electrification | 120V plug-in and low-amperage HPWHs for no-rewire retrofits | Only the big three incumbents ship 120V units: shared-circuit operation forces small compressors and slow recovery (physics), and UL/SKU economics favor scale manufacturers. Openings: variable-speed low-amperage designs with predictive controls (the gap Cala Systems targets), 120V units bundled with mixing valves and storage intelligence, and multifamily channel plays where panel capacity is the binding constraint. |
| Buildings | Water Heating & Appliance Electrification | Split-system and CO2-refrigerant HPWHs (residential) | Outside Japan only a couple of niche importers (ECO2 Systems, Reclaim) serve split-CO2 demand; $5-7k unit costs and installer unfamiliarity are the barriers. Tightening low-GWP refrigerant rules (A2L transition) create an opening for cost-reduced CO2 splits and for Japanese incumbents to localize Western residential product. |
| Buildings | Water Heating & Appliance Electrification | Central/commercial heat pump water heating plants (multifamily, hospitality, commercial) | Demand is code-driven (Washington and California all-electric and HPWH-credit codes) but nearly every job is custom-engineered with long design cycles. Opening for standardized packaged central-HPWH skids, sizing/design software, and heat-as-a-service offers that de-risk multifamily gas-boiler conversions. |
| Buildings | Water Heating & Appliance Electrification | Combined space + water heating heat pump systems with integrated thermal storage | US homes lack hydronic distribution and contractors treat combis as exotic, so only a handful of players exist despite combi dominance in Europe and Japan. Opening for packaged, forced-air-compatible combi kits and controls that let one heat pump serve HVAC + DHW with thermal storage as the load-shifting asset. |
| Buildings | Water Heating & Appliance Electrification | Drain-water heat recovery | The tech is mature, passive and cheap, yet adoption is tiny: retrofits need drain-stack access and save only tens of dollars a year per home, so no venture-scale company has formed. The realistic openings are code mandates (UK SAP, Canadian energy codes), bundling into multifamily central hot-water packages, and building-scale sewage heat recovery where SHARC is nearly alone. |
| Buildings | Water Heating & Appliance Electrification | Solar thermal and PV-diversion water heating | Global collector sales fell 14.2% in 2024 (REN21) as cheap PV plus heat pumps undercuts glycol plumbing; residential solar-thermal startups are effectively extinct in the West. The live opening is software-defined PV diversion, DC-coupled water heating, and PVT for space-constrained commercial loads — not new collector brands. |
| Buildings | Water Heating & Appliance Electrification | Induction and battery-integrated cooking (residential and commercial kitchen) | Battery-integrated cooking has exactly two venture-backed players, now locked in patent litigation — evidence the niche is real but fragile. UL certification cost, appliance retail margins and incumbent brand power deter entrants; the opening is licensing battery-stove IP to incumbent OEMs and 120V multifamily retrofit channels rather than new D2C hardware brands. |
| Buildings | Water Heating & Appliance Electrification | Heat pump clothes dryers, combo washer-dryers and commercial laundry | No startups here — appliance manufacturing scale and thin retail margins lock the category to incumbents. The genuine white space is commercial laundry electrification (laundromats and hotels remain overwhelmingly gas-dryer) where heat-pump retrofit economics, panel constraints and financing are unsolved as a package. |
| Buildings | Windows, Glazing & Solar Control | Electrochromic and other electrically dynamic smart glass | The 2024 collapse of View (>$2B consumed) and Halio ($564M) showed that dedicated EC fabs cannot survive construction-industry sales cycles; the opening is low-capex routes — retrofittable EC films, reversible-metal-electrodeposition chemistry, or licensing deposition onto existing float/coating lines — rather than another billion-dollar greenfield fab. |
| Buildings | Windows, Glazing & Solar Control | Thermochromic and passive-dynamic glazing | Effectively vacant after Pleotint suspended production and RavenWindow folded: passive tint tracks glass temperature rather than occupant intent (tinting on hot overcast days, lagging in winter sun) and offers no smart-building data story to justify premiums. The opening is commodity-priced thermochromic PVB bundled into standard laminating lines for hot-climate volume housing, where 'no controls' is a feature rather than a bug. |
| Buildings | Windows, Glazing & Solar Control | Window inserts, secondary glazing and low-e storm windows | Residential is well served, but commercial secondary glazing has only small players (Indow, Quanta) facing an enormous single-pane office stock under LL97-style carbon fines — the gap is scale-up capital, installer networks and financing models more than technology. |
| Buildings | Windows, Glazing & Solar Control | Transparent photovoltaic and energy-harvesting glazing | Physics caps fully transparent PV at tens of W/m², and Ubiquitous Energy's collapse despite Andersen's backing shows VC-scale economics don't close on vision glass alone; the viable openings are semi-transparent applications (spandrels, skylights, greenhouses, balustrades) where transparency can be traded for output, or licensing coatings into existing IGU lamination lines rather than building fabs. |
| Buildings | Windows, Glazing & Solar Control | Automated exterior shading, screens and dynamic facades | Crowded and mature in Europe but structurally absent from North American volume construction — no installer base, hurricane/wind-code friction, and aesthetics resistance; an opening exists for a US-focused, code-compliant integrated window+exterior-shade product or utility-program channel, as DOE attachment studies show exterior shading beats most glazing upgrades on cost per ton of cooling. |
| Buildings | Windows, Glazing & Solar Control | Transparent aerogel and super-insulating glazing inserts | Only a handful of players because supercritical drying of clear, crack-free aerogel monoliths is slow and capex-heavy at window sizes — transparency-grade optics is much harder than industrial aerogel blankets; whoever industrializes continuous or ambient-pressure drying for clear monoliths unlocks triple-pane performance retrofittable into the existing double-pane manufacturing base. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | Biochar & Bio-oil | Hydrothermal carbonization of wet wastes (hydrochar) | Hydrochar is less recalcitrant than pyrolysis char so registries award few or no durable-CDR credits, leaving plants to live off sludge tipping fees; EU bans on sludge landspreading and phosphorus-recovery mandates create a utility-partnered niche that remains thinly served — an opening for waste majors (Veolia/SUEZ-style) to roll up the licensors. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | Biochar & Bio-oil | Bio-oil and bio-slurry deep-well injection for permanent storage | Charm is still the only company injecting pyrolysis bio-oil at scale — bio-oil is corrosive, costs run several hundred dollars per tonne, and injection-well permitting is slow — so the opening is regional pyrolysis hubs feeding shared disposal wells and oilfield-services incumbents (who already drill and permit these wells) offering 'storage as a service' to any bio-oil producer. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | Biochar & Bio-oil | Biochar-embedded materials (concrete, asphalt, plastics, filtration media) | Construction codes and conservative mix-design specs make adoption slow, and biochar must beat near-free fillers like fly ash on cost; Made of Air's stall shows the product risk — the opening is code-approved, EPD-backed admixtures sold through ready-mix incumbents that stack embodied-carbon reductions with CDR credit revenue. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | Biochar & Bio-oil | Fast-pyrolysis bio-oil production for fuels and chemicals | Pyrolysis oil as fuel struggles against fossil parity and depends on a handful of refinery co-processing partners, which is why the plant fleet has barely grown since 2015; the visible opening is redirecting output to higher-value uses — carbon-removal injection feedstock (Charm's model) or specialty chemicals — rather than competing in heating fuel. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | Biochar & Bio-oil | Metallurgical and industrial biocarbon (fossil carbon substitution) | Steel, ferroalloy and silicon demand for fossil-coal substitutes dwarfs current biocarbon supply, but smelter qualification cycles and securing multi-decade fiber supply are brutal for startups — openings favor forestry incumbents and licensors who control feedstock (Weyerhaeuser's entry is the tell), and a credible European/Asian counterpart to Aymium is still missing. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | Biomass Storage & BECCS | Waste-to-energy CO2 capture (biogenic fraction) | Few movers because WtE-CDR depends on accurate biogenic-fraction accounting, CO2 transport/storage networks (e.g. Northern Lights), and willingness to retrofit municipal utilities; an opening exists for capture-tech vendors and aggregators who can standardize biogenic MRV and bundle many mid-size municipal plants. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | Biomass Storage & BECCS | Biogas/RNG upgrading CO2 capture for removal credits | The vented CO2 is biogenic and already separated, so capture is technically easy, but each digester emits only small, dispersed volumes that are uneconomic to truck to storage individually; the opening is aggregation models and onsite mineralization (e.g. into concrete) that avoid long-distance CO2 transport. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | Biomass Storage & BECCS | Biogenic CO2 mineralization into building materials (added) | Still a thin field: it requires both a biogenic CO2 source and nearby mineral-waste/recycling feedstock, and credit volumes per site are small; the opening is co-locating mineralization at construction-and-demolition recycling hubs in dense urban markets where CO2 transport distance is minimal. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | CO2 Transport & Geologic Storage | Rail, barge, and truck CO2 logistics | Few credible CCS-dedicated players because rail/truck costs ~2-4x pipeline at scale, so the mode stays niche (<100 ktpa sources); the opening is standardized LCO2 railcar fleets and transload terminals serving stranded ethanol/biogas plants in the post-Navigator Midwest, and inland-barge feeder systems into Rotterdam/Antwerp hubs — currently served only by food-grade incumbents with no pure-play startup. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | CO2 Transport & Geologic Storage | CO2 injection well services and specialty well engineering | Almost no pure-play startups: oilfield incumbents already own the rigs, cements and metallurgy, and CCS well counts (~10s/year) are too small to support independents yet. Openings exist in Class VI-specialist drilling contractors, CO2-rated P&A/legacy-wellbore remediation around storage complexes (every depleted-field project must fix old wells), and independent CO2 flow metering/verification as 45Q audit scrutiny rises. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | CO2 Transport & Geologic Storage | Novel storage media (in-situ mineralization, CO2 hydrates, enhanced coal-bed storage, engineered caverns) | Outside mineralization, this is open field: CO2 hydrate storage has zero commercial players (only academic work and PFLOTRAN modeling through 2025) because formation kinetics, monitoring and deep-sea logistics are unsolved; enhanced coal-bed storage died with its 2000s pilots (swelling kills injectivity); salt caverns are unsuitable for permanent CO2 (creep/economics favor storing H2 instead). The realistic opening is geography-driven mineralization — basalt/peridotite provinces in Iceland, Oman/UAE, Kenya, India's Deccan and offshore Cascadia — co-sited with DAC, where conventional saline basins do not exist; a credible hydrate demonstration in Arctic/deepwater sediments would be genuinely novel but needs patient capital. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | CO2 Utilization: Built Materials | Synthetic aggregates from carbonated industrial residues | Economics hinge on being paid to take hazardous waste (gate fees) plus carbon revenue — Carbon8's collapse shows equipment-licensing without owning the waste relationship struggles; the opening is operator models co-located at EfW/steel sites outside the UK, where regulation still sends most APC residue to landfill. |
| Carbon Removal & Capture | CO2 Utilization: Built Materials | Flue-gas direct mineralization to salable minerals (sodium bicarbonate, PCC, magnesium carbonates) | Salable-mineral markets (bicarbonate, PCC) are small and price-sensitive relative to CO2 volumes — Calera proved product revenue alone rarely pays for capture; viable openings are co-location with free CO2 and free alkalinity (steel slag, WtE residue, cement CKD) or high-purity specialty grades, not commodity scale. |

| Transport | Commercial, Heavy-Duty & Off-Road EVs | Agricultural tractors & farm equipment | Field agriculture is brutally hard for batteries (multi-day harvest peaks at 200+ hp, remote fields, thin dealer/service networks), and the VC route just failed publicly — Monarch and Solectrac both collapsed despite ~$300M combined raised — leaving incumbents to stage 75-130hp launches. The opening is depot-based specialty fleets (orchard, vineyard, dairy, municipal) with swappable packs, autonomy-bundled TCO, and low-cost compacts for India/Africa rather than head-on row-crop competition. |
| Transport | Commercial, Heavy-Duty & Off-Road EVs | Truck & bus repower/retrofit conversions | The graveyard here (pepper, Lightning eMotors, Lunaz, SEA, e-troFit) shows the economics: one-off engineering and certification on aging chassis can't beat falling new-BEV prices in rich markets. The credible openings are standardized kits for captive high-utilization fleets whose chassis outlive powertrains (refuse, terminal tractors, transit buses), retrofit-friendly regulatory regimes (France's décret rétrofit, India), and battery-swap conversions like Janus where charging infrastructure is the binding constraint. |
| Transport | Commercial, Heavy-Duty & Off-Road EVs | Electrified trailers & range-extending add-ons | Very few credible players because powered trailers sit in a regulatory grey zone (weight allowances, braking rules) and the industry splits incentives — tractor owners buy fuel while trailer owners buy trailers. An opening exists for per-mile leased range-extension that leaves the tractor and its warranty untouched, bridging fleets to electrification years before Class 8 BEVs and megawatt charging are ubiquitous. |
| Transport | Commercial, Heavy-Duty & Off-Road EVs | Battery-swap heavy trucks & swap ecosystems | Outside China this approach is nearly empty (Australia's Janus Electric is the lone notable player) because swap needs pack standardization across OEMs — a chicken-and-egg no western truck maker will start. The opening is corridor consortia (drayage lanes, mining/port shuttles) adopting a standard pack with battery-as-a-service financing, potentially importing CATL's proven 75# model. |
| Transport | EV Charging & Grid Integration | Megawatt charging systems (MCS) for heavy trucks | Hardware now exists, but multi-megawatt grid interconnections take 3-7 years in the US/EU — the opening is in battery-buffered or flexible-interconnect MCS hubs and in financing structures that de-risk utilization before electric long-haul truck volumes arrive. |
| Transport | EV Charging & Grid Integration | Battery swapping (cars and 2/3-wheelers) | Passenger-car swapping only works where one player controls pack standardization at scale — China's CATL/NIO advantage; the Western opening is not consumer cars (Ample's failure confirms it) but standardized commercial fleets and 2/3-wheeler markets in emerging economies. |
| Transport | EV Charging & Grid Integration | Wireless/inductive charging & electric road systems | Physics and cost keep this thin: inductive transfer adds $/kW and efficiency loss versus a cable, and no OEM factory-fits at volume; the credible opening is hands-free static charging for autonomous and transit fleets where eliminating charging labor justifies the premium — not consumer cars or long highway segments (Sweden postponed its permanent e-road). |
| Transport | EV Charging & Grid Integration | Battery-buffered, off-grid, solar-canopy & mobile chargers | Demand charges and multi-year interconnection queues create the niche, but hardware capex plus battery degradation crushed FreeWire even with $200M+ raised — the viable models pair the battery with a second revenue stack (ADS-TEC's ancillary services, Jolt's ad financing) rather than selling boxes alone. |
| Transport | EV Charging & Grid Integration | Curbside & pole-mounted urban charging | Outside the UK this is wide open: US curbside barely exists because utility make-ready costs and curb permitting kill L2 economics — models that bypass interconnection via building or streetlight power (itselectric, Voltpost) are the wedge, and no one yet owns the US city-franchise playbook. |
| Transport | EV Charging & Grid Integration | Robotic & automated charging | Almost nobody is at commercial stage here — Rocsys is nearly alone — yet the demand driver is compounding: robotaxi fleets (Waymo et al.) still charge with human labor, and autonomous trucking depots will need hands-free megawatt charging; a focused player that standardizes robot-ready connectors with OEMs could own the category. |
| Transport | Electric Two/Three-Wheelers & Micromobility | Electric motorcycles (on-road and off-road) | Player count is high but financial credibility is thin: Western consumer demand never materialized at premium prices (Energica, Cake, Fuell, Sondors all failed 2023-25) because range/price economics at highway speed favor ICE; the proven openings are profitable performance niches with vertical integration (Stark model) and low-cost commuter bikes in motorcycle-first markets (India, SE Asia, LatAm). |
| Transport | Electric Two/Three-Wheelers & Micromobility | Cargo e-bikes & electric delivery trikes/quads | The van-replacement e-quad niche remains thin: hardware is capex-heavy, fleet procurement cycles are slow, and quality failures (Babboe) show the durability bar; the opening is fleet-grade serviceable vehicles bundled with financing/maintenance (the Zoomo-style as-a-service model) rather than one-off hardware sales. |
| Transport | Electric Two/Three-Wheelers & Micromobility | Two-wheeler electric conversion & retrofit kits | Genuinely sparse: per-model homologation (RTO/ARAI type approval) is slow and expensive, kit-plus-battery cost approaches a cheap used EV, and liability/battery certification scares installers — so only a handful of credible players exist; the opening is B2B fleet retrofits (delivery fleets, boda-boda cooperatives, municipal fleets) where one approved model covers thousands of identical vehicles and TCO math is decisive. |
| Transport | Electric Two/Three-Wheelers & Micromobility | Emerging-market electric moto-taxis (Africa & SE Asia commercial two-wheelers) | Few players have scaled beyond one country because the model is triple-capital-intensive (vehicles + energy infrastructure + rider credit) in weak-currency markets; the opening is OEM partnerships with dedicated financiers/energy providers and pan-African battery standardization rather than fully vertically integrated plays. |
| Transport | Electric Two/Three-Wheelers & Micromobility | Standing e-kickscooters & personal light EV OEMs | Consumer kickscooter manufacturing is locked up by Chinese electronics-scale incumbents competing on price, leaving little room for hardware startups; remaining openings are regulatory-compliance niches (UL 2272-certified batteries, EU type approval) and durable fleet-spec hardware with repairability guarantees. |
| Transport | Maritime Decarbonization | Green methanol vessels, engines & fuel supply | Ships have outrun fuel: one commercial e-methanol plant (42 kt/yr) versus megaton-scale fleet demand, and developers die without bankable offtakes (Liquid Wind bankruptcy, Ørsted's FlagshipONE cancellation). The opening is for producers who can lock long-term offtakes and shrink the green premium, plus FuelEU-compliance structures that monetize it. |
| Transport | Maritime Decarbonization | Ammonia propulsion & ammonia-to-power | Engine supply is consolidating with the three incumbent engine houses; the open gaps are port-side bunkering networks, crew safety/training systems and N2O abatement — whoever industrializes safe ammonia transfer owns the bottleneck. |
| Transport | Maritime Decarbonization | Hydrogen & fuel-cell vessels | Two flagship failures (Future Proof Shipping, TECO 2030) plus Norled's heavy losses show green-hydrogen cost and missing infrastructure kill the economics wherever batteries suffice; the defensible niche is routes too long for batteries with captive or subsidized hydrogen supply — or onboard methanol reforming that dodges hydrogen logistics entirely. |
| Transport | Maritime Decarbonization | Primary-sail cargo shipping lines | Small capacity and ~11-knot speeds cap the addressable market to premium, time-tolerant cargo; FuelEU/CSRD-driven demand for verified low-CO2 freight is the opening, but nobody has cracked container-scale sail economics yet — Veer and France's Windcoop are first attempts. |
| Transport | Maritime Decarbonization | Onboard carbon capture | Capture is proven but the loop is open: there is no standardized port CO2 offload/storage chain and IMO/EU accounting for captured CO2 is still pending, so value hinges on regulation — first movers locking port logistics and cement/CCU offtake (the Seabound-Heidelberg model) own the gap. |
| Transport | Maritime Decarbonization | Nuclear maritime propulsion | Physics is proven by navies, but civilian port access, insurance/liability frameworks and IMO rules don't yet exist, so credible players are few and capital-heavy; the near-term opening is floating nuclear power for ports and islands (regulated like land assets) before propulsion, plus licensing/class-rule tooling. |
| Transport | Passenger & Light-Duty EVs | Solar-integrated EVs | Both flagship solar-car startups failed (Lightyear bankrupt, Sono's Sion canceled) and Aptera is still pre-revenue after years — physics caps on-vehicle PV near 1 kW, so a standalone 'solar carmaker' only pencils with extreme lightweighting. The credible opening is solar as a feature: OEM roof options, retrofit kits for fleets, and sun-rich-market microcars/LSVs where 20-40 km/day of free range covers most trips. |
| Transport | Passenger & Light-Duty EVs | Microcars, quadricycles & low-speed EVs | Crowded in Europe and China but nearly empty in the US, where no federal quadricycle class exists and the 25 mph low-speed-vehicle rule strands products between golf carts and cars — a regulatory void that helped kill Arcimoto and ElectraMeccanica. An opening exists for whoever cracks US homologation (a 35-45 mph 'medium-speed' class or state-level carve-outs) with a sub-$12k product like Eli's. |
| Transport | Passenger & Light-Duty EVs | EV skateboard platforms & contract manufacturers | Pure skateboard startups keep dying (Canoo Ch7 2025, Arrival 2024) and contract plants keep losing anchors (Magna-Fisker, VDL-MINI) because credible volume customers barely exist — incumbents own factories and startups die before reaching volume. The model that works is Foxconn's: regional assembly inside tariff walls plus white-label platforms for legacy brands that lack EV R&D (Mitsubishi-Foxtron), and Chinese platform licensing (Geely SEA, CATL) is the likely consolidation path. |
| Transport | Passenger & Light-Duty EVs | ICE-to-EV passenger conversions & classic-car retrofits | Mass-market retrofit has repeatedly failed (Transition-One liquidated despite French subsidies and a regulation written around it) because kit-plus-labor cost rivals a used BEV; only luxury restomods clear margins. The opening is standardized, type-approved kits for a handful of iconic high-volume models installed through OEM dealer networks (the Renault/R-FIT pattern), or subsidy-backed fleet retrofits where duty cycles are predictable. |
| Transport | Rail, Hydrogen for Transport & E-fuels | Autonomous battery railcars & rail freight innovation | Effectively a two-startup US field: FRA certification, railroad operating-rule conservatism, and Class I labor agreements make entry brutal, while the prize (the ~70% of US freight ton-miles on trucks) is huge. Openings exist for European/Asian equivalents on lighter-regulated networks and for retrofit propulsion/automation kits for existing railcar fleets. |
| Transport | Rail, Hydrogen for Transport & E-fuels | Locomotive repowering & hybrid retrofits | Thin field for zero-emission repowers: railroads sweat 30+ year assets and CARB's in-use locomotive rule was withdrawn from EPA waiver consideration in early 2025, gutting the regulatory driver. An opening exists for financing-wrapped repower-as-a-service priced against fuel savings rather than emissions mandates. |
| Transport | Rail, Hydrogen for Transport & E-fuels | E-fuels & low-carbon road fuels (e-gasoline, e-diesel, rDME) | Few players because green-H2 + DAC economics put e-gasoline at multiples of fossil prices, and most e-fuel capital is being diverted to aviation where SAF mandates guarantee demand. The EU's 2035 e-fuels carve-out for combustion cars creates a future certified e-gasoline supply-chain niche (certification, blending, distribution) that is nearly empty today. |
| Transport | Rail, Hydrogen for Transport & E-fuels | Freight logistics optimization & intermodal shift software | General freight software is crowded, but software purpose-built for road-to-rail modal shift (carbon-priced routing integrated with rail/intermodal capacity APIs) is nearly empty — most TMS tools optimize within trucking. A credible intermodal-shift engine pairing Class I capacity with shipper Scope 3 targets is an open seam. |
| Transport | Rail, Hydrogen for Transport & E-fuels | Hydrogen passenger cars (light-duty FCEVs) | Retail FCEV cars are in retreat — Shell closed all California light-duty stations in 2024 and Mirai resale values cratered — because chicken-and-egg station economics fail at low vehicle density. The viable opening is captive fleets with depot fueling (taxis, government fleets) as HysetCo demonstrates, not retail. |
