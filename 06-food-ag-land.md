## Food, Agriculture & Land Use

Roughly a third of global emissions once land use is counted: alternative proteins, livestock and rice methane, fertilizer, precision and regenerative agriculture, food waste, forests, and blue carbon. Solutions here are unusually behavior- and policy-dependent, and the alt-protein shakeout of 2023–2025 is a live case study in hype-cycle risk.

### Plant-Based & Fermentation Proteins

**Plant-based meat (ground, whole-cut, 3D-printed)** · `🟡 scaling`

Replicates ground, formed, or whole-cut meat from plant proteins (soy, pea, wheat) using extrusion, shear-cell, or 3D-printing to mimic muscle texture, cutting the land, water, and emissions footprint of livestock by roughly an order of magnitude.

**Plant-based dairy, cheese & yogurt** · `🟡 scaling`

Recreates milk, cheese, and yogurt from plant bases (oat, soy, nut, potato) plus plant proteins/fats and cultures, avoiding the methane and land use of dairy herds while targeting the harder functional challenge of meltable, stretchy cheese.

Replaces eggs (mung-bean or other plant-protein liquid eggs) and fish/shellfish (konjac, soy, legume, algae) with plant analogues, easing pressure on caged hens and on overfished and bycatch-heavy wild stocks.

**Mycoprotein & filamentous-fungi / biomass fermentation** · `🟡 scaling`

Grows whole filamentous fungal biomass in fermenters (the cells themselves are the food), yielding a fibrous, high-protein, high-fiber ingredient that is the most cost-mature fermentation route to meat-like texture and is economically viable at industrial scale today.

**Animal-free fats & oils (fermented or synthetic)** · `🟠 demonstration`

Produces fats and oils, dairy-style butterfat, meat fat, and palm-oil replacements, either by fermenting microbes (precision/biomass) or by building triglycerides thermochemically from captured carbon, addressing the fat fraction that drives much of the flavor, melt and deforestation impact of animal and palm products.

### Livestock & Rice Methane

**Enteric feed additives — seaweed / Asparagopsis** · `🟡 scaling`

Feeding cattle small amounts of red seaweed (Asparagopsis), whose active compound bromoform inhibits the methanogen enzyme that makes methane in the rumen, can cut enteric methane by 50-90% and matters because enteric fermentation is agriculture's single largest methane source.

### Fertilizer & Crop Inputs

**Nitrogen-fixing microbes / biological nitrogen fixation** · `🟡 scaling`

Engineered or naturally-selected bacteria applied to seeds or roots pull nitrogen from the air and feed it to non-legume crops like corn and wheat, replacing 20-50 lbs/acre of synthetic nitrogen and cutting the N2O and CO2 footprint of fertilizer manufacture and field loss.

| [⚠️ AKUA](https://civileats.com/2024/09/05/will-seaweeds-farming-fulfill-its-potential-as-a-climate-change-solution/) | startup | Kelp-burger and kelp-jerky consumer brand that tried to build retail demand for US-grown kelp. | Raised ~$4.5M (2020-24); shut down August 2024, assets taken over by a Maine farmer co-op. · **☠ shut down** |

> **White space:** The bottleneck is demand, not biology: US seaweed funding fell from ~$100M (2022) to ~$8M (2024) and consumer-food plays like AKUA failed. The durable opening is high-volume industrial offtake (biostimulants, feed, bio-materials), not consumer food brands.

**Seaweed feed additives for livestock methane reduction (Asparagopsis)** · `🟠 demonstration`

Growing red seaweed (Asparagopsis) or synthesizing its active compound bromoform to add to cattle feed, cutting enteric methane burps by up to ~90% and abating a potent greenhouse gas rather than storing carbon.

| Company | Type | What they do | Signal / status |
|---|---|---|---|
| [CH4 Global](https://ch4global.com/2025/01/29/ch4-global-begins-production-at-worlds-first-commercial-scale-asparagopsis-growing-facility-setting-new-benchmark-for-cost-effective-livestock-methane-reduction/) | scaleup | Opened the first commercial-scale Asparagopsis 'EcoPark' (Louth Bay, South Australia) producing the Methane Tamer feed additive via low-cost ponds. | Commercial production began January 2025; claims up to 90% methane cut and 90% lower production cost. |
| [Symbrosia](https://agfundernews.com/symbrosia-nets-5-8m-to-scale-most-cost-effective-cultivation-method-for-methane-busting-seaweed-additive) | startup | Land-based Asparagopsis cultivation (photobioreactor-to-pond) producing the SeaGraze additive; building what it bills as the world's largest land-based seaweed farm in Kona, Hawaii. | $5.8M Series A-1; FDA data submitted; 15-acre site targeted for mid-2027. |
| [Sea Forest](https://agfundernews.com/methane-busting-feed-supplements-are-beginning-to-scale-but-who-will-foot-the-bill-and-what-will-drive-widespread-adoption) | scaleup | Cultivates Asparagopsis in Tasmania for the SeaFeed oil and pellet supplement. | Raised ~$34M; operates a large Tasmanian seaweed farm. |
| [Blue Ocean Barns](https://www.pbs.org/video/cutting-methane-1744664894/) | startup | Grows Asparagopsis on land in filtered seawater (farm in Mexico) for the Brominata feed additive supplied to dairies. | Early commercial supply; one of the first US Asparagopsis additive makers. |
| [Rumin8](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/rumin8) | startup | Synthesizes and stabilizes bromoform pharmaceutically (no seaweed farming) for a low-cost, scalable feed additive. | ~$18M+ raised, including Breakthrough Energy / Bill Gates and Prelude Ventures. |
| [Volta Greentech](https://agfundernews.com/red-seaweed-was-billed-as-a-gamechanger-for-livestock-methane-reduction-so-when-will-it-deliver) | startup | Swedish maker that began with Asparagopsis but has pivoted away to an undisclosed, more-scalable methane-reducing molecule. | ~$2M seed; pivot away from Asparagopsis announced 2024-25. · *status unverified* |

> **White space:** Crowded on supply but the unsolved problem is who pays: there is no value chain that funds the per-animal premium, dosing for grazing herds is hard, and Asparagopsis bromoform is costly to grow at scale (hence Volta's pivot and Rumin8's synthetic route). An opening exists for whoever pairs cheap, stable dosing with a credible enteric-methane crediting/insetting mechanism.

**Seaweed / biomass carbon sequestration & sinking** · `🔵 pilot`

Growing or collecting macroalgae and sinking or burying the biomass (or converting it to biochar) so its carbon leaves the fast carbon cycle for centuries, a high-ambition but scientifically contested removal pathway.

| Company | Type | What they do | Signal / status |
|---|---|---|---|
| [⚠️ Running Tide](https://carbonherald.com/running-tide-shuts-down-citing-lack-of-demand-from-the-voluntary-market/) | startup | Flagship ocean carbon-removal firm that pivoted from kelp to sinking limestone-coated wood chips off Iceland and selling the removals as credits. | Raised >$50M (Lowercarbon et al.); sold credits to Microsoft and Shopify; shut down June 2024 citing collapsed voluntary-market demand. · **☠ shut down** |
| [Seafields](https://oceannews.com/news/science-technology/carbon-removal-company-completes-world-s-first-seaweed-bailing-and-sinking-test/) | startup | Plans open-ocean sargassum farms using artificial upwelling, then bales and sinks the biomass in the South Atlantic gyre. | Sank test bales at ~3,483 m in 2024 (with Running Tide and the Alfred Wegener Institute); pre-commercial, targeting the early 2030s. |
| [Carbon Kapture](https://carbonkapture.com/) | startup | Builds networks of kelp farms and converts harvested kelp into biochar for durable, on-land carbon storage. | Early UK/European pilots; participant in EU ocean-restoration projects. |
| [Brilliant Planet](https://carbonherald.com/brilliant-planet-lands-first-large-scale-carbon-removal-deal-via-watershed-marketplace/) | startup | Grows marine microalgae (not macroalgae) in coastal desert ponds, then solar-dries and buries the biomass on site. | >$25M raised (Toyota Ventures et al.); ~100 t/yr demo; sold removals to Block; targeting 270k t/yr by 2030. |

> **White space:** Thin and high-risk: open-ocean biomass sinking has no scientific consensus or accepted MRV, permitting is fraught (London Protocol/Convention), and the voluntary market shrank, which is exactly what killed Running Tide. The credible openings are on-land/biochar pathways and rigorous, measurable MRV rather than dumping biomass in the deep sea.

**Coastal / blue carbon MRV & credit methodologies** · `🟡 scaling`

The standards, measurement-reporting-verification (MRV) tooling and ratings that turn coastal-ecosystem carbon into credible, sellable credits, the financial plumbing the rest of the bucket depends on.

| Company | Type | What they do | Signal / status |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Verra](https://verra.org/programs/verified-carbon-standard/area-of-focus-blue-carbon/) | incumbent | Runs the VCS registry and the VM0033 tidal-wetland/seagrass methodology that certifies the large majority (~99%) of blue carbon issuance to date. | Dominant registry; blue carbon methodology revised 2023-2025. |
| [Isometric](https://www.eco-business.com/news/icvcm-approves-another-carbon-credit-standard-mangrove-restoration-rules-as-high-integrity/) | startup | Durable-CDR registry that published a Mangrove Restoration Protocol (Dec 2025) plus marine removal methodologies. | Three signed mangrove projects; potential to issue up to ~2M removal credits by 2030. |
| [Sylvera](https://www.sylvera.com/blog/sylvera-verra-vm0047-dmrv-performance-benchmark-data-provider) | scaleup | Carbon-credit ratings and Biomass-Atlas data; named a data-service provider for Verra's dMRV performance benchmark (VM0047). | Large VC-backed ratings platform; publishes the annual State of Carbon Credits report. |
| [BeZero Carbon](https://www.sylvera.com/blog/what-is-a-carbon-credit-agency) | scaleup | Carbon-credit risk-ratings agency; the only major rater to publish its topline ratings free. | Founded 2020; widely used by corporate buyers to screen blue-carbon credits. |
| [Calyx Global](https://calyxglobal.com/calyx-carbon-credit-ratings/) | startup | Independent carbon-credit ratings and risk analysis across 25+ project types, including blue carbon. | Founded 2021; subscription ratings service and detailed project reports. |
| [Pachama](https://www.sylvera.com/blog/voluntary-carbon-markets-ecosystem-explained) | scaleup | AI-powered MRV and marketplace for nature-based removals/avoidance and REDD+ credits. | Founded 2018; backed by Amazon Climate Pledge Fund and Lowercarbon. |
| [Plan Vivo](https://www.senken.io/academy/blue-carbon) | incumbent | Standards body running PV Blue, a community-focused blue carbon standard used by smallholder mangrove/seagrass projects. | Long-running standard with a dedicated blue-carbon module. |

**Shellfish / bivalve reef restoration** · `🟡 scaling`

Rebuilding oyster and mussel reefs that filter water, protect shorelines and create fish habitat, valuable coastal restoration whose role as a carbon sink is scientifically contested.

| Company | Type | What they do | Signal / status |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Oyster Recovery Partnership](https://www.oysterrecovery.org/our-work/oyster-restoration) | incumbent | Nonprofit that plans and plants science-based oyster restoration; completed Maryland's 10-tributary, 10-year restoration strategy in 2025. | Billions of spat-on-shell planted across the Chesapeake Bay. |
| [Billion Oyster Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster_reef_restoration) | startup | Nonprofit restoring oyster reefs in New York Harbor via public-school education and restaurant shell-recycling networks. | 100M+ oysters restored toward a one-billion-oyster goal. |
| [The Nature Conservancy](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/chesapeake-bay/oyster-reef-restoration-chesapeake-bay-were-making-significant-progress) | incumbent | Leads large global shellfish/oyster reef restoration programs and ran the SOAR shellfish-buyback initiative during COVID. | Dozens of reef restoration projects across the US, Europe and Australia. |
| [Puget Sound Restoration Fund](https://restorationfund.org/programs/olympiaoysters/) | startup | Nonprofit restoring native Olympia oysters and pinto abalone across Puget Sound. | 140+ acres of Olympia oyster habitat restored by early 2025. |
| [Chesapeake Bay Foundation](https://www.cbf.org/blogs/save-the-bay/2025/04/harnessing-the-little-bivalve-to-produce-big-results-for-the-bay.html) | incumbent | Runs oyster-gardening and reef-building programs across the Chesapeake with partner agencies. | Helped reach the 2025 10-tributary restoration goal. |

> **White space:** Almost no for-profit players, because the carbon case is weak: shell calcification releases CO2, so bivalves are excluded from blue carbon and mCDR frameworks and earn no credits, leaving restoration dependent on grants and public funds. An opening would require a defensible net-removal MRV (recent PNAS work hints at organic-carbon co-benefits) or stacking biodiversity, coastal-protection, water-quality and nutrient-trading credits into a revenue model.
